## 1. Alstom's recent sales, adjusted EBIT margin, backlog, gross margin in backlog, and what drives margin

### Takeaway
Alstom has a record backlog (€104.4bn at 31 March 2026) priced at an 18.0% gross margin, but it booked only 13.3% gross margin in the FY 2025/26 income statement and a 6.1% adjusted EBIT margin; management attributes the gap to rolling stock execution: long development phases, late homologation and "additional activities performed to implement engineering changes". The 8-10% margin ambition for FY 2026/27 was withdrawn in April 2026 and a new CEO (Martin Sion, from 1 April 2026) has made execution, "platforming & standardisation" and procurement savings the stated levers.

### Inferences
- Inference: the FY 2025/26 P&L gross margin of 13.3% equals adjusted gross margin €2,555m divided by sales €19,171m (13.33%). The same ratio for FY 2024/25 is 14.1% (€2,613m / €18,489m). So booked gross margin fell about 0.8 points year on year while the margin priced in the backlog rose.
- Inference: the slide-51 gap between the "theoretical" 16.0-16.5% and the booked 13.3% is 2.7 to 3.2 points. On €19,171m of sales that is roughly €520m to €610m of gross margin in one year attributed by Alstom to "project execution and industrial inefficiencies". This is my arithmetic on Alstom's own slide; Alstom does not publish the euro amount.
- Inference: one point of gross margin on FY 2025/26 sales is worth about €190m; the "(60) bps" for project execution in the EBIT bridge is about €115m.
- Inference: "additional activities performed to implement engineering changes" during ramp-up is the closest Alstom comes in public to naming late design change as a cost driver. It does not say how much of this is avoidable through reuse of existing designs. Linking reuse to this line is our argument, not Alstom's statement.
- Inference: because bid costs are expensed as selling costs and losses at completion are recognised immediately, both a wasted tender effort and a wrong tender cost estimate reach the P&L quickly and visibly.

### Gaps
- Alstom does not disclose margin by product line, by site or by contract, nor the number of contracts it classes as non-performing or legacy.
- FY 2023/24 full-year sales and aEBIT in euros were not captured in this session (only margin, backlog and FCF).
- No disclosure of tender win rate, number of tenders per year, or bid cost in euros (only that bid costs sit inside selling expenses of €359m in FY 2025/26).

## 2. What the URD risk factors say about tenders, contract execution, cost overruns, penalties, complexity and customisation

### Takeaway
The URD describes tender costing as an exercise done without detailed design, where "existing references may become obsolete over time, requiring redesign or adaptation for new applications" and actual costs "could significantly exceed the initial estimated costs at the time of bidding"; contract execution risk is rated by Alstom itself as remaining "high". The term "fixed-price contract" does not appear in the risk factors of either URD; the risk is expressed through cost-estimate, penalty and termination language instead.

### Cited Findings

All quotes in this section are from section 4.2.2 "Operational risks" of [URD 25/26](https://www.alstom.com/sites/alstom.com/files/2026/05/28/20260528_URD_Alstom_EN.pdf) unless marked URD 24/25. Read in full: 4.2.2.1 to 4.2.2.4.

**4.2.2.2 Tender management risk (PDF p185, printed p183)**

- Quote: "Much of the Group's business comes from public sector tender offer processes involving formal contracts with limited room for negotiation. This can result in Alstom entering into long-term contracts with less favourable conditions or more stringent requirements than those normally sought by the Group (e.g. multiple penalty clauses, limitations on liability, cash position)."
- Quote: "The Group must be able to define the costing assumptions at the time of bidding for all customer specifications while also proposing the best organisation within the Group for the successful execution of the contract. These assumptions may turn out to be inaccurate at execution time, in particular regarding the following aspects:"
- Quote (first bullet): "as the tender phase is not a detailed design phase, some technical assumptions and choices are made during tender about the ways to comply with customers' technical requirements. These may prove wrong at contract stage if changes are requested by the client to such choices or to the assumed list of suppliers, with potentially adverse impacts on cost, time or validation of performance;"
- Quote (second bullet): "existing references may become obsolete over time, requiring redesign or adaptation for new applications;"
- Quote (third bullet): "due to the nature of Alstom's products and solutions, the planned project organisation is often complex in terms of high number and coordination of stakeholders and sites being involved in design (various domains of competencies and expertise), sourcing (multiple suppliers in many countries), production (industrial organisation with several sites)."
- Quote: "Furthermore, the actual costs of developing and building projects could significantly exceed the initial estimated costs at the time of bidding. The risk of cost overruns is exacerbated by macroeconomic volatility including inflationary pressures, and component scarcity or cost inflation. Similarly, if it is impossible to achieve the relevant required performance or planned schedule, the customer can, in the majority of cases, demand the payment of penalties or terminate the contract."
- Quote: "The inability of Alstom to win contracts while effectively anticipating the required costs and capacity to execute them would have a material adverse effect on its business, results of operations, financial condition, and prospects."
- Mitigation, quote: "The tender review process is based on an in-depth risk analysis that includes a list of items that must be systematically reviewed and verified. These elements take into account various parameters, such as the customer's profile, the contractual organisation of the project and its partners, the project timetable, the solution novelty, contractual clauses, the exposure to exchange and inflation risks, country risks, tax issues, and key financial elements (contract price, margin, risks and opportunities, and related financial reserves, cash curves, etc.)."
- Mitigation, quote: "The application of this process relies on specific documentation, and an analytical and validation tool for all commercial opportunities, and ensures the traceability of assumptions applied throughout the entire cycle."
- Mitigation, quote: "Tender risk is also monitored based on specific monitoring of the transition period between tender and project phases, as well as a feedback loop making it possible to capitalise on best practices and lessons learned."
- Mitigation, quote: "The project management organisation that has been set up recently by sub-systems for the project execution phase, is also deployed during the tender phase to ensure the efficient and smooth transfer of information and data between the tender and the contract teams."

**4.2.2.1 Contract execution risk (PDF p184, printed p182)**

**Risk ranking**

- In the URD 24/25 risk map, "Contract Execution Risk", "Tender Management Risk", "Suppliers' Risk" and "Workforce and skills planning Risk" sit together at the high-impact, high-probability end of the list — [URD 24/25](https://www.alstom.com/sites/alstom.com/files/2025/05/28/20250528_Universal_Registration_Document_EN.pdf) PDF p38. ### Inferences
- Inference: the new URD 25/26 sentence about "existing references" becoming obsolete and "requiring redesign or adaptation for new applications" is Alstom acknowledging, in a regulated filing, that the reusability of past designs is a tender costing assumption that can fail. It is the single most useful sentence for the pilot's business case, because it ties design reuse to tender risk in Alstom's own words.
- Inference: the mitigation text describes a tender "analytical and validation tool" with "traceability of assumptions" and a sub-system-based organisation shared between tender and project teams. A sub-assembly reuse and costing view over PLM and ERP fits inside that stated control framework; the URD does not describe any tool that compares a tender's bill of materials to existing sub-assemblies.
- Inference: Alstom says penalties and termination are available to the customer "in the majority of cases". It gives no figure for penalties paid.

### Gaps
- The words "fixed-price" and "lump sum" do not appear in the risk factors of URD 25/26 or URD 24/25 (keyword search of the full text of both). The only "fixed price" hit in URD 25/26 concerns energy purchase contracts (PDF p46).
- "Customisation" is not named as a risk factor in either URD. The word appears mainly in product descriptions (signalling, turnkey metros).
- No quantification of cost overruns, penalties paid or loss-making contract count in either URD.
- Risk factors beyond 4.2.2.1 to 4.2.2.4 were keyword-searched, not read line by line.

## 3. What Alstom says about platforms, standardisation, modularity, reuse, design-to-cost, engineering hours and R&D

### Takeaway
Alstom publicly names "Platforming & Standardisation" and "Product convergence" as profitability levers for rolling stock, describes its product approach as "modular", and cited "standardisation of engineering tools and processes together with design to cost" as a margin driver in 2024. It publishes no design reuse rate, no parts commonality target and no engineering-hours metric.

### Cited Findings

- Rolling stock levers slide. Under "Profitability improvement levers", Alstom lists as business development levers "Selectivity", "Product convergence", "Acquisition value capture", "Increased % of projects indexed"; and as operational levers "Operational excellence", "Reduced quantity of assembly lines", "Digitalisation", "Platforming & Standardisation", "New standard manufacturing lines", "Footprint utilisation & BCC lever", with the stated outcome "Progressive margin uplift to high-single digit profitability" — [Equity Story Oct 2026](https://www.alstom.com/sites/alstom.com/files/2026/10/06/Alstom_Equity_Story_October_2026_EN.pdf) slide 15. ## 8. Consulting and industry studies on complexity cost, modularisation and standardisation in rolling stock

### Takeaway
I found no rail-specific consulting or industry study that quantifies complexity cost or the savings from modularisation in rolling stock. What exists is general engineered-to-order research (McKinsey, a Danish academic case) and market-size data (UNIFE); these can support the argument only by analogy.

### Cited Findings
- McKinsey, "How smart platforms can crack the complexity challenge in project industries": argues that modularisation, standardisation and design to value must be combined; recommends modules covering roughly 80% of target market segments with a limited number of variants, which reduces "the cost of complexity and at the same time product cost"; warns that the modular system "should not aspire to meet rare special requirements if they make it more complex and expensive than economically reasonable" — [McKinsey article](https://www.mckinsey.com/br/our-insights/how-smart-platforms-can-crack-the-complexity-challenge-in-project-industries) (consulting report; **search summary only**, the page and its PDF timed out three times).
- McKinsey, "Platforming and modularity: smart answers to ever-increasing complexity": cites a manufacturer that replaced more than 70 different hydraulic pumps with a portfolio of 20 "plug and play"-like solutions — [McKinsey article](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/operations/our-insights/platforming-and-modularity-smart-answers-to-ever-increasing-complexity) (consulting report; search summary only, page timed out).
- Academic case (peer-reviewed conference paper): Løkkegaard and Mortensen, Technical University of Denmark, ICED 2017. A global OEM with more than 50,000 product variants and USD 3.5bn turnover (2015) showed a potential to reduce its cost base by "up to 15%" by systematically sharing key design principles across "80% of the company's portfolio" — [Design Society, ICED17](https://www.designsociety.org/publication/39607/assessing_the_financial_potential_for_modularization_a_case_study_in_a_global_oem) (abstract only; the OEM is industrial equipment, not rail).
- UNIFE World Rail Market Study 2024 (10th edition, prepared with Bain & Company, not Roland Berger): global rail supply market averaged €201.8bn a year, up from €176.5bn in the 2022 edition; forecast about 3% real annual growth to an average €240.8bn. A 2026 edition exists in a digital modular format — [UNIFE WRMS page](https://www.unife.org/news-resources/wrms/) and [UNIFE news release](https://www.unife.org/news/showcasing-a-european-industry-on-the-cusp-of-dynamic-global-transformation-unife-at-innotrans/) (industry body; search summary only).
- Alstom's own use of the study: rolling stock market shares 2022-2024 "(WRMS 2024)" with Alstom at 25% of an addressable market averaging €38bn a year (2024-2026), and a 2.6% CAGR "based on UNIFE" — [Equity Story Oct 2026](https://www.alstom.com/sites/alstom.com/files/2026/10/06/Alstom_Equity_Story_October_2026_EN.pdf) slide 12. The slide is a flattened chart; the 25% and €38bn labels should be checked visually.
- Roland Berger's rail publications found are about financing (88% of European rolling stock procurement is publicly funded or underwritten, 2016) and supply-industry consolidation, not platforms — [Railway Suppliers summary of the Roland Berger / Rail Working Group study](https://railwaysuppliers.ca/news/european-governments-still-fund-88-of-rolling-stock-procurement-2016-01-28) (search summary only).
- A vendor marketing page (Quick Release, a PLM consultancy) states that tailoring rail platforms "to the needs of different networks, power systems, operators and signalling systems creates high levels of variant complexity that can lead to delayed deliveries and commissioning difficulties" — [Quick Release, rail](https://quickrelease.co.uk/QR-for/rail) (vendor marketing; search summary only).

### Inferences
- Inference: the absence of rail-specific quantified studies is itself useful for the use-case document. Any percentage saving we quote for rolling stock will be either Alstom's own numbers (sections 1 and 4) or an analogy from other engineered-to-order industries, and should be labelled as such.
- Inference: the 88% public funding figure explains why tenders are formal with "limited room for negotiation" (section 2): customers are public authorities bound by procurement law, which pushes specification variety onto suppliers.

### Gaps
- Not found: any McKinsey, Roland Berger, BCG, Oliver Wyman or SCI Verkehr publication that quantifies complexity cost, modularisation savings or reuse rates in rolling stock. Searches for each returned nothing citable.
- The two McKinsey articles could not be opened (timeouts); everything attributed to them is from search result summaries and needs a direct read before quoting.
- The UNIFE WRMS full study is paid; only headline figures from press material were seen. Whether it discusses standardisation is unknown.
- The DTU paper was seen as abstract only.

## 9. Known loss-making contracts where design or customisation issues were cited

### Takeaway
The best-documented cases are Bombardier Transportation's 2019-2020 charges (about US$350m, then US$435m) for "incremental engineering, certification and retrofit costs" on late-stage UK and German projects, Alstom's follow-on provisions after the acquisition, and the Aventra-driven cash warning of October 2023. Regio 2N, built at Crespin, ran about eight months late with complexity and regional requests cited as causes.

### Cited Findings
- Bombardier Q2 2020, quote: "These results reflect an additional charge of $435 million at Transportation, largely related to incremental engineering, certification and retrofit costs associated with a number of late-stage projects mainly in the U.K. and Germany." ### Inferences
- Inference: Bombardier's own description, "incremental engineering, certification and retrofit costs", names late engineering change as the cost category. Sources do not say the root cause was customisation or lack of reuse; press coverage points to software certification and aggressive bidding. Use these cases as evidence that late engineering change is expensive, not as evidence that reuse would have prevented them.
- Inference: adding the two Bombardier charges (about US$785m over three quarters) and Alstom's €632m provision gives well over €1bn of recognised losses on inherited contracts. The amounts are in different currencies and periods and partly overlap in scope, so they should not be summed in a client document.
- Inference: the Regio 2N delay, with "requests from the regions" cited, is the only case found where customer-driven variant requests are named as a cause, and it happened at Crespin. The source is Wikipedia, so it needs a primary citation before use.

### Gaps
- Not found: any Alstom or Bombardier statement that explicitly attributes a contract loss to customisation or to failure to reuse an existing design.
- Not found: contract-level loss figures for Aventra, FV-Dosto, Talent 3 or Regio 2N.
- The €632m and €451m provisions were not verified in Alstom's FY 2020/21 URD (not downloaded); the Alstom press release URL tried returned 404.
- No named loss-making contract was found for Alstom's FY 2025/26 problems; Alstom does not name the "some large rolling-stock projects".
- Other candidates not researched: Alstom's Coradia Stream delays for NS and DSB, Avelia Liberty (Amtrak) delays, Siemens or Stadler problem contracts.
