# Rolling-stock tender process from the manufacturer's side, and where design reuse is decided

Research date: 2026-10-12. Scope: passenger rolling stock; France and Europe first, UK and Australia as supporting evidence.

Reading conventions used below:
- "Quote" means text I read myself in the extracted document (PDF text or page HTML). Location is given as page, paragraph or section.
- "(via fetch summary)" means the page was read through a summarising fetch tool, so wording is paraphrase unless marked, and should be re-checked before being quoted in a published document.
- "(search snippet only)" means the page was not opened.
- Anything under "Inferences" is my own reasoning, not a source statement.
- Read status and source grade for every source are in the last section.

## 1. What are the steps of a rolling-stock tender, from call for tenders to contract award and execution, and how long does each take?

### Takeaway
Public buyers run a staged procedure: market sounding, expression of interest or pre-qualification, shortlist, invitation to tender, bid, evaluation, negotiation with a preferred bidder (often with a best and final offer), contract award, then design, build, testing, authorisation and delivery. In the documented cases the bid window is about 3.5 to 6 months, call-to-contract takes 1.5 to 2 years when it goes to plan and 4.5 years when it does not, and first trains arrive 3.5 to 8 years after contract.

### Cited Findings

Stages (buyer-defined):
- The ARA framework lists the stages a supplier goes through: market sounding and pre-project engagement, pre-qualification, probity, early contractor involvement (ECI), agreement of terms and conditions, compliance statement, "from pre-qualification right through to contract award" (p.1; contents page). — [ARA Tendering Framework 2020](https://ara.net.au/wp-content/uploads/Tendering-Framework-2020-2.pdf)
- "The typical procurement process includes either a distinct pre-qualification phase or a requirement to supply equivalent information within the main Request for Proposal (RFP) document set" (p.3). — [ARA Tendering Framework 2020](https://ara.net.au/wp-content/uploads/Tendering-Framework-2020-2.pdf)
- Agreeing terms and conditions alone "can take up to six months" (p.5). — [ARA Tendering Framework 2020](https://ara.net.au/wp-content/uploads/Tendering-Framework-2020-2.pdf)
- A compliance statement against every buyer requirement is "an integral part of the submitted offer"; the supplier "must assure themselves that their obligations are fully understood and costed" (p.8). Tender specifications are "frequently narrative in style with multiple and interwoven requirements within a single paragraph", so requirements must be extracted by hand, and the exercise "must be formally repeated as one of the first post contract deliverables" (p.8). — [ARA Tendering Framework 2020](https://ara.net.au/wp-content/uploads/Tendering-Framework-2020-2.pdf)
- Arup's lifecycle for rolling stock (Figure 3.2, p.35): need and requirements (1-2% of whole-life cost), procurement process including "Options appraisal, Design, Competition, Transaction" (5-10%), vehicle production (30-60%), testing and commissioning (3-6%), operational period (40-70%), decommissioning (1-3%). — [Arup for the Rail Value for Money (McNulty) study, March 2011](https://www.orr.gov.uk/sites/default/files/om/rvfm-arup-rolling-stock-mar2011.pdf) (read from the Internet Archive copy of this URL)

Worked timeline 1, New South Wales New Intercity Fleet (NIF), 512 cars, A$1.3bn supply contract (NSW Audit Office, Oct 2025, sections 2 and 4.1):
- EOI issued 11 August 2014; seven responses; four shortlisted.
- RFT issued 4 September 2015; "responses were received on 23 December 2015".
- Tender Review Panel approved deeper discussions with the preferred tenderer on 27 April 2016; that tenderer "had 23 outstanding commercial departures".
- NSW Government approved award 3 August 2016; project deed executed mid-August 2016 "for the price of $1.3 billion".
- First trains were expected in 2019; "the first NIF trains entered passenger service ... on 3 December 2024".
- The RFT packages were: 512 cars "from a proven standard platform design", a driver simulator, maintenance equipment, and 15 years of maintenance services.
— [NSW Audit Office, Rail rolling stock procurement, 21 Oct 2025](https://www.audit.nsw.gov.au/our-work/reports/rail-rolling-stock-procurement)

Worked timeline 2, New South Wales Regional Rail Fleet (RRF), PPP with CAF as train supplier (same report, sections 2 and 5):
- EOI issued 21 August 2017, closed September 2017; three of four respondents shortlisted.
- RFP issued 15 December 2017; "initial responses were received from two respondents on 27 June 2018 (extended from 1 June 2018 and 22 June 2018)"; the third respondent withdrew on 16 May 2018.
- Proposals were evaluated "including submission of best and final offers".
- Contract executed 13 February 2019 with Momentum Trains (subcontract to CAF for rolling stock).
- Original completion date December 2022; revised to April 2026; "At the time of this report, services had not commenced."
— [NSW Audit Office, 2025](https://www.audit.nsw.gov.au/our-work/reports/rail-rolling-stock-procurement)

Worked timeline 3, UK Thameslink and Intercity Express (NAO, July 2014):
- Thameslink: invitation to tender November 2008, "estimating that it would take around 16 months to complete the procurement and award the contract. In fact it took over four and a half years" (contract June 2013) (para. in Part One, Figure 2).
- Intercity Express: department "originally expected to award the contract in April 2009, 17 months after issuing the invitation to tender in November 2007"; contract closed July 2012.
- Five expressions of interest were received for each; three full bids for Thameslink and two for Intercity Express (para 2.9).
- Preferred bidder to contract: Agility Trains (Hitachi) preferred February 2009, contract July 2012; Cross London Trains (Siemens) preferred June 2011, contract June 2013 (para 2.13).
- Thameslink bidders had to "revise their bids three times", delaying the process "by around ten months in total" (para 2.4).
— [NAO, Procuring new trains, HC 531, 9 July 2014](https://www.nao.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2014/07/Procuring-new-trains.pdf)

Worked timeline 4, France, Régiolis:
- SNCF coordinated a joint tender for the regions over about two years; framework signed 27 October 2009 (via fetch summary). — [Railway Gazette, 28 Oct 2009](https://railwaygazette.com/news/regions-confirm-7bn-framework-ter-contract/34484.article)
- Alstom's own release of 27 October 2009: "Les premières livraisons sont prévues dès 2013 et s'échelonneront jusqu'à mi 2015" for the firm tranche of 100 trainsets. — [Alstom press release, 27 Oct 2009](https://www.alstom.com/fr/press-releases-news/2009/10/SNCF-attribue-a-Alstom-le-marche-de-la-nouvelle-generation-de-materiels-TER-a-un-niveau-20091027)
- Deliveries of Régiolis and Regio 2N were reported about a year late in 2014 by the Aquitaine region (via fetch summary). — [Banque des Territoires / Localtis, 3 Mar 2014](https://www.banquedesterritoires.fr/materiel-ferroviaire-les-regions-veulent-reprendre-les-commandes)

Execution phase on the manufacturer side:
- Alstom runs "a specific process with several Gate Reviews on each project to progressively approve the successive progress of each project phase (process called Development for Quality)" and project reviews "on a regular basis" (section 4.2.2.1, p.174). — [Alstom Universal Registration Document 2023/24](https://www.alstom.com/sites/alstom.com/files/2024/05/15/20240515_Alstom_Universal_Registration_Document_EN.pdf)
- Alstom monitors "the transition period between tender and project phases" and deploys a programme "to ensure the efficient and smooth transfer of information and data between the tender and the contract teams" (section 4.2.2.2, p.175). — [Alstom URD 2023/24](https://www.alstom.com/sites/alstom.com/files/2024/05/15/20240515_Alstom_Universal_Registration_Document_EN.pdf)

### Inferences
- Derived durations (my arithmetic from the dates above): NIF bid window about 3.5 months, EOI-to-contract about 24 months, contract-to-first-service about 8 years 4 months against about 3 years planned. RRF bid window about 6 months (including extensions), EOI-to-contract about 18 months. Régiolis contract-to-first-delivery about 3.5 to 4 years as planned.
- A reasonable planning figure for the reader: 3 to 6 months to prepare a bid, 1.5 to 2 years from call to contract, 3 to 4 years from contract to first delivery for a platform-derived train. This is a synthesis of four cases, not a published benchmark.
- The requirement extraction that ARA describes (done once for the bid, repeated after award) is a point where the same engineering analysis is performed twice. That is where a requirements-to-BOM traceability tool would sit.

### Gaps
- No source found giving a standard, manufacturer-published stage-gate map of a tender (bid/no-bid, solution freeze, price approval) with durations. Alstom's filing confirms the reviews exist but not their names or timing.
- No French-specific official timeline for a recent regional train tender (after 2020) was found.
- The NSW report gives the NIF contract award as "August 2016" in section 2 and "September 2016" in section 4.4; the inconsistency is in the source.

## 2. Who is involved on the manufacturer side and the buyer side, and what does each do during a bid?

### Takeaway
Alstom's filing describes the functions that take part (tender team, finance, commercial organisation, a tiered tender review up to the Board for the largest bids) but no independent source describes the individual roles; role detail found is from job postings only and was not opened in full. On the buyer side the roles are well documented: transport authority, operator, financier or owner, advisers, probity adviser.

### Cited Findings

Manufacturer side (company filing, Alstom):
- "The tender review process is based on an in-depth risk analysis that includes a list of items that must be systematically reviewed and verified", covering "the customer's profile, the contractual organisation of the project and project partners, the project timetable, contractual clauses, the exposure to exchange and inflation risks, country risks, tax issues, and key financial elements (contract price, margin, risks and opportunities and related financial reserves, cash curves, etc.)" (section 4.2.2.2, p.175). — [Alstom URD 2023/24](https://www.alstom.com/sites/alstom.com/files/2024/05/15/20240515_Alstom_Universal_Registration_Document_EN.pdf)
- "The review process for commercial offers includes several control steps up to the submission of the tender to the customer. Depending upon how complex and risky the opportunity is, the audience of the reviews is adjusted to obtain the proper approvals" (p.175). — [Alstom URD 2023/24](https://www.alstom.com/sites/alstom.com/files/2024/05/15/20240515_Alstom_Universal_Registration_Document_EN.pdf)
- The process "relies on a specific documentation, analytical and validation tool for all commercial opportunities, and ensures the traceability of assumptions applied throughout the entire cycle" (p.175). — [Alstom URD 2023/24](https://www.alstom.com/sites/alstom.com/files/2024/05/15/20240515_Alstom_Universal_Registration_Document_EN.pdf)
- Board level: the Board "is also informed of the most significant current invitations to tender, for which a presentation of the strategy and risks is submitted to it" (corporate governance chapter, p.233). — [Alstom URD 2023/24](https://www.alstom.com/sites/alstom.com/files/2024/05/15/20240515_Alstom_Universal_Registration_Document_EN.pdf)
- Finance: "The Finance function defines the Group's principles and financial policies in terms of tenders and projects control"; Group Business Performance runs the rules "to plan, monitor and evaluate financial performances of tenders, projects, functions, regions and product lines" (pp.191-192). — [Alstom URD 2023/24](https://www.alstom.com/sites/alstom.com/files/2024/05/15/20240515_Alstom_Universal_Registration_Document_EN.pdf)
- Commercial: the Central Commercial Organization is in charge of a "well-balanced order intake across all regions and product lines with the right level of profitability" and aims "to early anticipate and mitigate risks in the commercial process"; the commercial pipeline tool is named "Wall-C" (p.361). — [Alstom URD 2023/24](https://www.alstom.com/sites/alstom.com/files/2024/05/15/20240515_Alstom_Universal_Registration_Document_EN.pdf)
- Engineering is involved at bid stage: the filing mentions "engineering working on the tendering" in its training section (line reference only; page not recorded) and "a management of our customer requirements initiated in tender phase and continued during the execution of the project" (p.174). — [Alstom URD 2023/24](https://www.alstom.com/sites/alstom.com/files/2024/05/15/20240515_Alstom_Universal_Registration_Document_EN.pdf)
- Bid manager duties described in Siemens Mobility job postings (search snippet only, vendor material): leads the tender lifecycle from reviewing technical scope and contract terms to bid strategy; is the link to engineering, purchasing, manufacturing and compliance; prepares a clause-by-clause compliance matrix and gap analysis; maintains a risks and opportunities register; works within a fixed bid budget. — [Siemens job posting 490064](https://jobs.siemens.com/en_US/externaljobs/JobDetail/490064); [Siemens job posting 109428](https://jobs-sandbox.siemens.com/en_US/externaljobs/JobDetail/109428)

Buyer side:
- France: "les régions fixent le plan de transport et la politique tarifaire. Elles financent l'acquisition du matériel roulant" while SNCF Mobilités operates the lines (pp.20-21). — [Cour des comptes, Les transports express régionaux à l'heure de l'ouverture à la concurrence, Oct 2019](https://www.ccomptes.fr/system/files/2019-10/20191023-rapport-TER-ouverture-concurrence.pdf)
- France: "les régions n'achètent pas directement les trains mais subventionnent SNCF Mobilités, qui achète les trains et en reste propriétaire"; "en 2017, SNCF Mobilités était propriétaire de 87 % du parc de matériel roulant" (pp.26-27). — [Cour des comptes 2019](https://www.ccomptes.fr/system/files/2019-10/20191023-rapport-TER-ouverture-concurrence.pdf)
- France: SNCF drafts the specification, negotiates with the manufacturers (Alstom and Bombardier at the time), runs the purchase and handles after-sales (via fetch summary). — [Banque des Territoires, 3 Mar 2014](https://www.banquedesterritoires.fr/materiel-ferroviaire-les-regions-veulent-reprendre-les-commandes)
- UK: vehicles are "specified either by Government agencies or by train operating companies and then purchased from manufacturers by leasing companies, who then hire the trains to the TOCs". — [Rail Magazine, Taking stock of how we procure trains, 17 Dec 2014](https://railmagazine.com/features/taking-stock-of-how-we-procure-trains) (read from Internet Archive snapshot of 18 Jul 2025)
- UK: the Department for Transport ran Thameslink and Intercity Express itself and "made extensive use of consultants" for specifications, procurement requirements and contracts (summary para 8). — [NAO 2014](https://www.nao.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2014/07/Procuring-new-trains.pdf)
- NSW: the buyer used a Tender Review Panel, an independent probity adviser, an EOI Evaluation Plan and a Tender Evaluation Plan; the operator (NSW Trains) sat on governance committees and "heavily influenced" refinement of the concept design during contract finalisation (sections 4.1, 5.1, 5.4). — [NSW Audit Office 2025](https://www.audit.nsw.gov.au/our-work/reports/rail-rolling-stock-procurement)
- Buyers limit the shortlist partly to protect bidders: TfNSW policy is to shortlist a maximum of three "to 'ensure appropriate competitiveness and to minimise the cost to industry of tendering'" (section 4.1). — [NSW Audit Office 2025](https://www.audit.nsw.gov.au/our-work/reports/rail-rolling-stock-procurement)

### Inferences
- Mapping of the roles named in the brief onto what the sources support (inference, not sourced role by role): bid or tender manager owns the schedule, compliance matrix and risk register (job postings); system engineers analyse the specification and define the offered solution (ARA p.8, Alstom "customer requirements initiated in tender phase"); cost estimators and finance build "contract price, margin, risks and opportunities and related financial reserves, cash curves" (Alstom p.175); procurement prices bought-in sub-systems (no direct source); senior management and, for the largest bids, the Board approve (Alstom pp.175, 233).
- No source read names the "engineering director" as approver of the technical solution. That is plausible but unverified.

### Gaps
- No independent or peer-reviewed description of a rolling-stock bid team and its division of labour was found. The University of Twente master's thesis on platform development at Bombardier Transportation (2014) looked relevant but its record page would not load, so it was not read.
- Alstom's 2024/25 filing (the latest) could not be text-extracted (font encoding), so the 2023/24 edition was used. Its risk matrix headings show the risk is now titled "Tender Management Risk"; body text not read.

## 3. How much does bidding cost a manufacturer, and what share of bids are lost?

### Takeaway
The only quantified figures found are Australian: tendering costs of about 1-2% of project cost against a 0.5% world benchmark, and bids "often running to many millions of dollars". No source gave a per-bid euro figure for a European train tender or a manufacturer win rate.

### Cited Findings
- "The tendering costs in Australia are estimated to be around 1-2% of a project's total cost, at least double the world benchmarks of 0.5%" (p.1; the footnote attributes this to Rail Express, "The Sustainability of Rail Contracting in Australia", 2012). — [ARA Tendering Framework 2020](https://ara.net.au/wp-content/uploads/Tendering-Framework-2020-2.pdf)
- "For the supplier, the costs of tendering such a project are particularly large, often running to many millions of dollars" (p.10, section "Cost of Procuring Rolling Stock"). — [ARA Tendering Framework 2020](https://ara.net.au/wp-content/uploads/Tendering-Framework-2020-2.pdf)
- Because follow-on orders are uncertain, "all tender engineering costs must be applied to the initial order" (p.10). — [ARA Tendering Framework 2020](https://ara.net.au/wp-content/uploads/Tendering-Framework-2020-2.pdf)
- Recommendation 21: where two or more bidders contest new rolling stock, "a stipend should routinely be provided to the non-successful tenderers to aid in cost recovery of the design costs associated with tendering" (p.10). — [ARA Tendering Framework 2020](https://ara.net.au/wp-content/uploads/Tendering-Framework-2020-2.pdf)
- "Increased tender costs immediately reflect in project pricing" (p.1). — [ARA Tendering Framework 2020](https://ara.net.au/wp-content/uploads/Tendering-Framework-2020-2.pdf)
- ECI payments to bidders are "typically well below the fair market value of that work" (p.5). — [ARA Tendering Framework 2020](https://ara.net.au/wp-content/uploads/Tendering-Framework-2020-2.pdf)
- Railway Gazette's report of the framework: ARA made "21 recommendations", including "cost recovery mechanisms for unsuccessful bidders for rolling stock contracts requiring design work"; ARA's chief executive said "Australian tendering costs are higher than global benchmarks". The article adds no figures beyond the framework. — [Railway Gazette, 15 May 2020](https://railwaygazette.com/business/industry-calls-for-improvements-to-australian-rail-tendering-processes/56508.article)
- UK: delay raised bid costs: "there will also have been increased costs for all the bidders, as a result of them having to keep bid teams together for long periods of time" (para 2.33). — [NAO 2014](https://www.nao.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2014/07/Procuring-new-trains.pdf)
- UK: on Intercity Express, "train manufacturers and the Department spent time and money producing and evaluating bids for routes which were then excluded" (para 2.2). — [NAO 2014](https://www.nao.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2014/07/Procuring-new-trains.pdf)
- UK: Arup refers to "costs associated with a high level of abortive bids" (p.61) and "abortive bid costs" caused by changes in government policy (p.68), without a figure. — [Arup 2011](https://www.orr.gov.uk/sites/default/files/om/rvfm-arup-rolling-stock-mar2011.pdf)
- Number of competitors as a proxy for loss rate: NIF had seven EOI responses, four shortlisted, one winner; RRF four, three shortlisted, two bids; Thameslink five expressions, three bids; Intercity Express five, two bids. "it is unusual for fewer than three bidders to tender for rolling stock orders in the UK" (Arup p.61). — [NSW Audit Office 2025](https://www.audit.nsw.gov.au/our-work/reports/rail-rolling-stock-procurement); [NAO 2014](https://www.nao.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2014/07/Procuring-new-trains.pdf); [Arup 2011](https://www.orr.gov.uk/sites/default/files/om/rvfm-arup-rolling-stock-mar2011.pdf)

### Inferences
- Order-of-magnitude illustration only: applying ARA's 0.5% to 2% range to the firm Régiolis tranche of EUR 800m would give EUR 4m to 16m of tendering cost. The ARA percentage is quoted for "a project's total cost" and it is not clear whether it covers one bidder or all parties, so this must not be presented as a fact about Alstom.
- With three or four shortlisted bidders and one winner, a bidder with average odds loses two of three or three of four shortlisted bids. This is arithmetic from the shortlist sizes, not a measured win rate.

### Gaps
- NOT FOUND: any published bid cost in euros or pounds for a specific European rolling-stock tender.
- NOT FOUND: any manufacturer-disclosed win rate or hit rate (Alstom, Siemens Mobility, Stadler, CAF). Alstom's filing gives book-to-bill ratios, which are not win rates.
- The 0.5% world benchmark traces to a 2012 trade-press article (Rail Express) that I did not locate; treat it as an industry-association claim.
- The Deloitte Access Economics 2013 report that ARA cites for whole-life cost shares was not obtained (see section 6 for what the search snippet reported).

## 4. How are offers priced: fixed price, recurring versus non-recurring cost, contingency, penalties?

### Takeaway
Bids are committed prices built at the time of bidding from costing assumptions for every customer requirement, with margin, risk reserves and cash curve reviewed before submission; contracts carry penalty clauses for delay and performance. Non-recurring (design and engineering) cost is a large and separately identifiable driver: Arup estimates per-vehicle cost is 20% to 60% higher for orders under 200 vehicles because of it.

### Cited Findings
- "Much of the Group's business comes from public sector tender offer processes involving formal contracts with limited room for negotiation", leading to "more stringent requirements than those normally sought by the Group (e.g., multiple penalty clauses, limitations on liability, cash position)" (section 4.2.2.2, p.175). — [Alstom URD 2023/24](https://www.alstom.com/sites/alstom.com/files/2024/05/15/20240515_Alstom_Universal_Registration_Document_EN.pdf)
- "The Group must be able to define at the time of bidding the costing assumptions for all the customer's specifications" (p.175). — [Alstom URD 2023/24](https://www.alstom.com/sites/alstom.com/files/2024/05/15/20240515_Alstom_Universal_Registration_Document_EN.pdf)
- "the actual costs of developing and building projects could significantly exceed the initial estimated costs at the time of bidding" and if performance or schedule is missed "the customer can in the majority of cases, demand the payment of penalties or terminate the contract" (p.175). — [Alstom URD 2023/24](https://www.alstom.com/sites/alstom.com/files/2024/05/15/20240515_Alstom_Universal_Registration_Document_EN.pdf)
- Elements reviewed before a bid goes out: "contract price, margin, risks and opportunities and related financial reserves, cash curves" (p.175). — [Alstom URD 2023/24](https://www.alstom.com/sites/alstom.com/files/2024/05/15/20240515_Alstom_Universal_Registration_Document_EN.pdf)
- Inflation: "new rules impacting both cost and sales terms have been put in place in an effort to reduce the Group's exposure to the inflationary context" (p.175). — [Alstom URD 2023/24](https://www.alstom.com/sites/alstom.com/files/2024/05/15/20240515_Alstom_Universal_Registration_Document_EN.pdf)
- Currency during the bid: Alstom hedges tenders submitted in foreign currency "depending on the probability to obtain the" contract (financial statements, notes on currency risk; page not recorded). — [Alstom URD 2023/24](https://www.alstom.com/sites/alstom.com/files/2024/05/15/20240515_Alstom_Universal_Registration_Document_EN.pdf)
- Non-recurring cost: "costs per vehicle can be between 20%-60% higher for orders of less than 200 vehicles (because of non-recurring costs)" (p.47). — [Arup 2011](https://www.orr.gov.uk/sites/default/files/om/rvfm-arup-rolling-stock-mar2011.pdf)
- "Prescriptive rolling stock requirement is reported by most industry parties to drive higher design costs for manufacturers. It also impedes the ability of the industry to produce more standard products" (p.48). — [Arup 2011](https://www.orr.gov.uk/sites/default/files/om/rvfm-arup-rolling-stock-mar2011.pdf)
- Share of design in whole-life cost: "planning and design typically accounting for 20% of whole of life costs, even for trains based on proven platforms, and the other 30% incurred during the build" (p.10, citing Deloitte Access Economics 2013). — [ARA Tendering Framework 2020](https://ara.net.au/wp-content/uploads/Tendering-Framework-2020-2.pdf)
- Priced options: in the RRF tender "Proponents were required to price" the bi-mode option in their proposal; the buyer exercised it after award, and it makes up "around 65% of the total cost of the approved variations" (section 5.5). — [NSW Audit Office 2025](https://www.audit.nsw.gov.au/our-work/reports/rail-rolling-stock-procurement)
- Add-on orders cost more per unit: 42 additional NIF cars were bought in February 2019 "at an increased cost of around 28% per car" (section 4.4). — [NSW Audit Office 2025](https://www.audit.nsw.gov.au/our-work/reports/rail-rolling-stock-procurement)
- Framework with firm tranche and options (France): first Régiolis order of 100 trainsets, "La tranche ferme de ce contrat s'élève à 800 millions d'euros", with intended options for 35 more. — [Alstom press release, 27 Oct 2009](https://www.alstom.com/fr/press-releases-news/2009/10/SNCF-attribue-a-Alstom-le-marche-de-la-nouvelle-generation-de-materiels-TER-a-un-niveau-20091027)
- Risk allocation complaint from suppliers: "contractors are exposed to some risks over which they have little or no control", including "changes to design standards midway through a project" (p.6). — [ARA Tendering Framework 2020](https://ara.net.au/wp-content/uploads/Tendering-Framework-2020-2.pdf)
- Buyer-side exposure to late delivery (not supplier penalties): the UK department estimated its liabilities if infrastructure is late at "up to £0.8 million per day for the Great Western fleet and £1.0 million per day for the East Coast fleet" (Part Three). — [NAO 2014](https://www.nao.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2014/07/Procuring-new-trains.pdf)

### Inferences
- Firm tranche EUR 800m for 100 trainsets implies about EUR 8m per Régiolis trainset in 2009 prices, including the non-recurring development for the new platform. My division; the split between recurring and non-recurring is not published.
- Because non-recurring engineering is loaded on the firm order (ARA p.10) and can add 20-60% per vehicle on small orders (Arup p.47), the reuse rate assumed at bid time moves the price directly. This link is my inference from the two sources; neither states it in those words.

### Gaps
- NOT FOUND: typical contingency percentages, penalty rates per day of late delivery or penalty caps in European rolling-stock contracts.
- NOT FOUND: any published breakdown of a train bid price into recurring and non-recurring cost.
- Whether the Régiolis and Regio 2N frameworks use fixed prices with indexation formulas was not confirmed in any source read.

## 5. At what step, and by whom, is it decided to reuse an existing design, adapt it, or design new? What information is needed?

### Takeaway
No source read states explicitly who makes the reuse decision or at which gate. The evidence shows it is constrained twice: first by the buyer's specification (which may demand a "proven standard platform" or may over-specify), then by the manufacturer during bid preparation, when the offered product is defined and every requirement is costed. Alstom's filing identifies the two failure conditions that matter for reuse: the product changing after award, and reference projects being too old to be relevant.

### Cited Findings
- Buyer can mandate reuse: the NIF tender asked for "512 cars from a proven standard platform design for intercity operation" (section 2). — [NSW Audit Office 2025](https://www.audit.nsw.gov.au/our-work/reports/rail-rolling-stock-procurement)
- Requirements nevertheless kept moving: NIF design requirements "evolved iteratively over the course of the procurement", with "further refinements ... made during the RFT, contract negotiation and award stages" (section 4.2). — [NSW Audit Office 2025](https://www.audit.nsw.gov.au/our-work/reports/rail-rolling-stock-procurement)
- Late customer change example: on RRF "there was a late change (in Q4, 2018) to the requirements for a second crew seat", from a flip-down seat to a full second driver's seat, during contract finalisation (section 5.4). — [NSW Audit Office 2025](https://www.audit.nsw.gov.au/our-work/reports/rail-rolling-stock-procurement)
- Manufacturer's bid-time obligation: define "the costing assumptions for all the customer's specifications while also proposing the best organisation within the Group for the successful execution of the contract" (p.175). — [Alstom URD 2023/24](https://www.alstom.com/sites/alstom.com/files/2024/05/15/20240515_Alstom_Universal_Registration_Document_EN.pdf)
- Conditions under which those assumptions fail, as listed by Alstom (p.175): "modification of technical definitions during the execution phase (change in the product being offered in order to comply with the customer's requirements)"; "projects have not been undertaken over a certain period and have limited relevance for new applications"; "complexity in the planned organisation (organisation of the project's design, or procurement, industrial organisation of the project)". — [Alstom URD 2023/24](https://www.alstom.com/sites/alstom.com/files/2024/05/15/20240515_Alstom_Universal_Registration_Document_EN.pdf)
- Alstom's stated control: a tool that "ensures the traceability of assumptions applied throughout the entire cycle" and "a feedback loop making it possible to capitalise on best practices and lessons learned" (p.175). — [Alstom URD 2023/24](https://www.alstom.com/sites/alstom.com/files/2024/05/15/20240515_Alstom_Universal_Registration_Document_EN.pdf)
- Compliance analysis is where the fit between an existing product and the specification is tested: the supplier may answer "Fully Comply but in the Stated Manner", i.e. comply using its own existing solution (Recommendation 18, p.8). — [ARA Tendering Framework 2020](https://ara.net.au/wp-content/uploads/Tendering-Framework-2020-2.pdf)
- Standard responses reduce bid effort: harmonised specifications would reduce "tendering costs and time through common reusable responses" (p.6). — [ARA Tendering Framework 2020](https://ara.net.au/wp-content/uploads/Tendering-Framework-2020-2.pdf)
- Platform as the reuse vehicle (France): Régiolis is Alstom's Coradia Polyvalent, "Doté d'une grande modularité, ce train régional à un niveau offre plusieurs types de configurations techniques et d'aménagements pour les passagers". — [Alstom press release, 27 Oct 2009](https://www.alstom.com/fr/press-releases-news/2009/10/SNCF-attribue-a-Alstom-le-marche-de-la-nouvelle-generation-de-materiels-TER-a-un-niveau-20091027)
- Under the Régiolis framework, individual regions specify and procure their own trains within the platform: three lengths (56 m, 72 m, 110 m), electric or bi-mode traction, and suburban, regional or inter-city interiors (via fetch summary). — [Railway Gazette, 28 Oct 2009](https://railwaygazette.com/news/regions-confirm-7bn-framework-ter-contract/34484.article)
- Customer-specific variants erode platform reuse: "TOC-specific requirements ... may include different vehicle lengths and door configurations. One manufacturer stated that there are now 20 variants of its stock being maintained in the UK" (p.48); buyers obtain fewer "benefits from what otherwise might be standard platforms for vehicles" (p.52 area; page approximate). — [Arup 2011](https://www.orr.gov.uk/sites/default/files/om/rvfm-arup-rolling-stock-mar2011.pdf)
- "greater use of modular and off-the-shelf products are cited as being attractive" (p.50). — [Arup 2011](https://www.orr.gov.uk/sites/default/files/om/rvfm-arup-rolling-stock-mar2011.pdf)
- Alstom presents interior innovations as going into "more than 2/3rd of our Rolling Stock Tenders" and a lighting solution as "progressively becoming the standard solution for most of our rolling stock platforms" (p.15). — [Alstom URD 2023/24](https://www.alstom.com/sites/alstom.com/files/2024/05/15/20240515_Alstom_Universal_Registration_Document_EN.pdf)

### Inferences
- Likely decision sequence (inference): (1) at bid/no-bid, commercial and platform management choose which platform to offer; (2) during bid preparation, system engineers run the requirement-by-requirement compliance analysis against that platform and classify each sub-system as carry-over, adapt or new; (3) cost estimators price each class, with adapt and new carrying non-recurring engineering and risk reserve; (4) the tender review approves the resulting price, margin and reserves. Steps 2 and 3 are where reuse is decided in practice; step 4 is where it is ratified. Only step 4 is directly evidenced (Alstom p.175).
- Information needed at step 2 and 3, derived from Alstom's two failure conditions and ARA's compliance discussion (inference): which existing sub-assemblies satisfy each requirement; in which past project and variant they were last built and how recently; their actual (not estimated) recurring cost; their authorisation status; and the delta engineering needed where the fit is partial.
- Alstom's wording "projects have not been undertaken over a certain period and have limited relevance for new applications" is the closest a company filing comes to saying that stale or hard-to-find reference designs lead to wrong bid costs. This is the strongest single citation for the pilot's problem statement.

### Gaps
- NOT FOUND: an explicit statement of who decides reuse versus new design in a rolling-stock bid, or of the data used. This remains inference.
- NOT FOUND: any quantified reuse rate (share of parts or sub-assemblies carried over) between variants of Coradia Polyvalent, Omneo or any other platform.
- Peer-reviewed literature on platform reuse at tender stage was searched for and not found; the only academic hit (Twente thesis, Bombardier, 2014) could not be opened.

## 6. What goes wrong: underestimated cost or scope, over-customised specifications, fleet diversity from bespoke tendering?

### Takeaway
Independent audits document large overruns and delays tied to scope not fixed at tender, late requirement changes and under-scoped enabling works; French and UK public bodies both document fleet fragmentation (39 TER series in France in 2018; 46 designs in Britain in 2004) and its cost. Alstom itself reports margin loss from contracts inherited from Bombardier.

### Cited Findings

Underestimated cost and scope:

## 7. How does French regional train procurement work (SNCF Voyageurs, Régions, framework contracts, opening to competition)?

### Takeaway
Historically the regions paid, SNCF specified, tendered, bought and owned: two national framework contracts (Régiolis with Alstom in 2009, up to 1,000 trainsets; Regio 2N with Bombardier, now Alstom, up to 860) let regions draw down configured variants through options. With opening to competition, regions are taking ownership of fleets and depots, and the Senate warned in 2026 that this risks fragmenting train models unless orders are coordinated.

### Cited Findings
- Roles in 2019: regions set the service plan and fares and "financent l'acquisition du matériel roulant" (pp.20-21); they "n'achètent pas directement les trains mais subventionnent SNCF Mobilités, qui achète les trains et en reste propriétaire (même si les clauses de reprise par les régions ont été précisées par la loi pour un nouveau pacte ferroviaire)" (pp.26-27). — [Cour des comptes 2019](https://www.ccomptes.fr/system/files/2019-10/20191023-rapport-TER-ouverture-concurrence.pdf)
- Scale: rolling-stock investment of about "3,3 Md€" between 2012 and 2017; average fleet age fell "de 16 à 12 ans entre 2012 et 2016" (pp.26-27). — [Cour des comptes 2019](https://www.ccomptes.fr/system/files/2019-10/20191023-rapport-TER-ouverture-concurrence.pdf)
- Régiolis award: "SNCF confirme la première commande de 100 rames (tranche ferme) assortie d'une intention d'options de 35 rames supplémentaires"; destined for eight regions (Alsace, Aquitaine, Basse-Normandie, Haute-Normandie, Lorraine, Midi-Pyrénées, Pays de la Loire, Picardie); "Ce marché, financé par des subventions versées par les Régions françaises". SNCF's president thanked the regions for their trust "à toutes les étapes de conception, de conseil et de négociation de cet appel d'offres". — [Alstom press release, 27 Oct 2009](https://www.alstom.com/fr/press-releases-news/2009/10/SNCF-attribue-a-Alstom-le-marche-de-la-nouvelle-generation-de-materiels-TER-a-un-niveau-20091027)
- Régiolis framework: up to 1,000 trainsets, potentially more than EUR 7bn over 10 years; first 135 trains split across eight regions (Midi-Pyrénées 25, Alsace 22, Aquitaine 22, Basse-Normandie 18, Picardie 17, Pays de la Loire 15, Haute-Normandie 8, Lorraine 8); about 60% bi-mode; assembly at Reichshoffen (via fetch summary). — [Railway Gazette, 28 Oct 2009](https://railwaygazette.com/news/regions-confirm-7bn-framework-ter-contract/34484.article)
- Regio 2N / Omneo: framework for up to 860 trains; 567 ordered across ten regions; 500th delivered from Crespin in June 2026; lengths 81 to 135 m; contract value not disclosed (via fetch summary; this outlet's article contained unrelated filler figures, so treat it as low reliability and re-verify against an Alstom release). — [Railway News, 15 Jun 2026](https://railwaynews.net/alstom-delivers-500th-omneo-regio2n-train-in-france.html)
- Regions' dissatisfaction, 2014: regions had acquired 1,500 trains for EUR 5.8bn since 2002; they "n'en sont pas propriétaires"; nine authorities created an "association d'étude sur le matériel roulant" on 21 February 2014 to consider pooled purchasing; seven regions had suspended payments over late deliveries (via fetch summary). — [Banque des Territoires, 3 Mar 2014](https://www.banquedesterritoires.fr/materiel-ferroviaire-les-regions-veulent-reprendre-les-commandes)
- Opening to competition requires authorities to "traiter la question de la propriété des matériels roulants et des centres de maintenance" and takes "plutôt cinq ans pour les premiers appels d'offres" (p.86). — [Cour des comptes 2019](https://www.ccomptes.fr/system/files/2019-10/20191023-rapport-TER-ouverture-concurrence.pdf)
- 2026 position: "les régions décident souvent de se faire transférer la propriété du parc de matériel roulant des TER"; this "est source de désoptimisation puisqu'auparavant, SNCF Voyageurs mutualisait la gestion de ses rames à une échelle plus étendue" (p.4). — [Sénat synthesis note 2026](https://www.senat.fr/fileadmin/cru-1778089154/Commissions/Finances/2025-2026/Controles/Controle_concurrence_ferroviaire__essentiel_post_commission.pdf)
- 2026 recommendation no. 4: the State, with the regions, "doit établir une stratégie nationale visant à définir les besoins futurs en termes de matériel roulant" and enable authorities "de passer des commandes groupées aux fabricants" (p.2). — [Sénat synthesis note 2026](https://www.senat.fr/fileadmin/cru-1778089154/Commissions/Finances/2025-2026/Controles/Controle_concurrence_ferroviaire__essentiel_post_commission.pdf)
- 2026: TER lots awarded after competition show production cost reductions "fréquemment entre 20 % et 30 %" (p.3). — [Sénat synthesis note 2026](https://www.senat.fr/fileadmin/cru-1778089154/Commissions/Finances/2025-2026/Controles/Controle_concurrence_ferroviaire__essentiel_post_commission.pdf)
- Search snippets only (not opened): competitive tendering of regional contracts becomes the rule from 25 December 2033; Nouvelle-Aquitaine and Occitanie created an inter-regional public company (SPIIT) to manage their fleet. — [FNAUT bulletin, 2024](https://www.fnaut.fr/uploads/2024/08/B-3999_p31_Fnaut.pdf); [Région Nouvelle-Aquitaine notice, 28 Oct 2024](https://www.nouvelle-aquitaine.fr/sites/default/files/2024-10/Avis_de_conclusion_et_de_consultation_publie_28_10_2024.pdf)

### Inferences
- For a manufacturer the French regional market has been one tender (the framework) followed by many configured call-offs per region. Each call-off is a small "variant bid": the platform is fixed, but length, traction, interior and equipment vary. This matches the pilot's framing of reuse across variants better than a from-scratch tender does.
- Valenciennes link: Regio 2N / Omneo is built at Crespin (near Valenciennes) per the 2026 trade report; Régiolis was assembled at Reichshoffen per the 2009 report. Which Alstom site the pilot's "Valenciennes" refers to (Petite-Forêt or Crespin) is not established by these sources.
- As regions become owners and buyers, the number of distinct contracting authorities a supplier faces rises from one (SNCF) towards a dozen, each with its own specification unless grouped orders happen. Inference from the Senate note.

- NOT FOUND: who tendered against Alstom in 2009 in a source I read in full (a search snippet says Bombardier).
- NOT FOUND: a primary legal source read in full for the ownership-transfer rules (2018 pacte ferroviaire, 2019 mobility law) or for the 2033 date; only secondary snippets.
